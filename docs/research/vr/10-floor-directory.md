# 10. The floor directory by the stairwell door (US, 1975-1980)

Question: what a visitor saw next to the stairs of a 10+ storey US university building around
1975-1980: the sign listing the floors and what is on each. Time-boxed research (about 10
minutes, 10.10.2026). Marks as in `07-corridor-1979.md`: **opened**, **search**, **unverified**.

## 1. Form

- **Changeable letter directory board.** Tablet & Ticket Co. (Chicago) registered a trademark
  in 1948 for "building directory boards and changeable letter signs" [F1, search]. So the
  changeable-letter building directory existed well before 1979.
- Modern catalogs still sell the same build: felt grooved background (also cork or magnetic),
  extruded aluminum frame 1-3/8 in face, 2 in deep, surface or recessed mount (Nelson-Harkins
  240 series) [F2, search]; felt grooved on 1/4 in centres for single-tab white injection-moulded
  letters; enclosed cabinets with locking door and glass or acrylic window (Aarco) [F3, search].
  These are modern pages: that the 1979 board looked the same is **unverified** until a period
  catalog or photo is found.

## 3. Stairwell floor identification (standards)

- Later NFPA 101 (2009, 7.2.2.5.4.1): a sign at each floor landing inside enclosed stairs
  serving five or more storeys (new: three or more), giving floor level, top and bottom terminus,
  stair identification, and the level and direction of exit discharge; readable with the door
  open or closed [F4, search]. IBC-based local codes add 18 x 12 in minimum, floor number at
  least 5 in high [F5, search]. Whether NFPA 101-1976 had this rule: **unverified** so far.
