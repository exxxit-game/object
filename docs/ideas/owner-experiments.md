## Two phones, two prices (10.10)
"Two phones lie on a table with a price sign beside each: one a very expensive phone, ten thousand
dollars, the other ten dollars. Which will you take? It is a virtual phone, not a real one, yet I think
people will take the expensive one. What interests me is how they will justify their choice: that they
can call better with it."

The interest is the reason given after the choice, not the choice. Published work to find and read before
any design (names to check, nothing taken from memory yet): reasons people give for a choice they did not
make for those reasons (Nisbett & Wilson, "Telling more than we can know", 1977); choice blindness
(Johansson et al. 2005, card docs/cards/johansson-2005.md); price as a cue to quality.
