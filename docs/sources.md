## Room 01 — Ono (1987)

Ono, K. (1987). Superstitious behavior in humans. *Journal of the
Experimental Analysis of Behavior, 47*(3), 261–271. doi:10.1901/jeab.1987.47-261
Full text (scanned pages): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1348309/

### Method (verified, full text)
| Fact | Page |
|---|---|
| 20 undergraduates (6 men, 14 women), aged 19–24, none had been in an experiment before | 262 |
| Booth 500 cm wide, 250 cm deep, behind a partition | 262 |
| Three steel levers 120 cm apart (centre to centre) on a 70 cm high table; frame 28.5 × 36 × 34 cm; 10 N to operate; a red lamp on each frame lit on every effective pull | 262–263 |
| On the wall facing the subject, 90 cm above the table: a four-digit counter and a small red reinforcer lamp; a signal light 5 cm below the lamp | 263 |
| Schedules: fixed-time or variable-time, 30 s or 60 s (FT30, FT60, VT30, VT60); VT30 intervals 3–57 s, VT60 25–95 s; 5 subjects per schedule, randomly assigned | 263 |
| Each point: added to the counter with a flash of the reinforcer lamp and a buzzer, independent of responding | 263 |
| Session 40 min: 30 min of points, then 10 min of extinction (no points) | 263 |
| Signal light: red, orange or green, each lit 6–8 times in random order; equal total duration and equal number of points under each colour | 263 |
| Instruction by tape recorder (in Japanese): "You may not leave the experimental booth or come out from behind the partition during the experiment. The experimenter does not require you to do anything specific. But if you do something, you may get points on the counter. Now try to get as many points as possible." | 263 |
| Observed from the next room through a one-way mirror; afterwards a questionnaire about how points were produced, then told the purpose | 263 |
| A point within 1 s after a pull counted as contiguous (PCR) | 267 |

## Latané & Darley (1968), JPSP 10(3), 215–221 (full text read 07.10.2026)
Archive copy: https://web.archive.org/web/2020id_/http://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/5248/f73d3cf3ce2696ccc241d89c9b2538fd1896.pdf
- Male Columbia students invited to an "interview" on urban university life; sent to a small waiting room to fill in a preliminary form (p. 217). Room size not given; it had a window participants opened (p. 218); observer behind a one-way window covered by a sign.
- Smoke (titanium dioxide) through a small wall vent after the participant finished two pages; by the end vision was obscured (p. 217, fn. 2).
- Conditions: alone n = 24; two passive confederates n = 10 (glance, shrug, back to the form, "I dunno"); three naive participants, 8 groups (p. 217).
- Stopped 6 min after the participant first noticed the smoke (p. 217); noticing judged by a slight start (p. 219).
- Results: alone 75% reported (18/24), median within 2 min; 55% by 2 min, 75% by 4 min (pp. 217–218). Passive confederates 1/10 (10%). Naive triads: someone reported in 3/8 groups (38%) vs > 98% expected; 1 of 24 people within 4 min (p. 218). Noticing within 5 s: 63% alone vs 26% in groups (p. 219).
- Debrief: non-reporters explained the smoke away (steam, air conditioning, "smog", "truth gas") and said they had not been influenced by the others (pp. 219–220).
- VR replication Kinateder & Warren 2016, Front. Robot. AI 3:43 (alarm at minute 10, n = 150): left the room — alone 68% real / 36% VR; passive neighbour 12% / 24%; active neighbour 92% / 60%.

## Alloy & Abramson (1979), JEP: General 108(4), 441–485 (full text read 07.10.2026)
Archive copy: https://web.archive.org/web/2019id_/http://www.morilab.net/gakushuin/Alloy_&_Abramson_1979.pdf
- Apparatus: black stand 23×23 cm with a yellow and a green lamp; black box 15.5×7.5×4 cm with a spring button (p. 450).
- Trial: yellow lamp starts a 3 s window to press or not; then the green lamp comes on or not. 40 trials; gaps 10–25 s, mean 14 s (p. 451). Instruction text pp. 451–452; experimenter left the room. Rating 0–100 in steps of 5: No / Intermediate / Complete Control (p. 451).
- Exp. 1 (n = 96): contingent tasks; everyone judged control accurately (p. 453).
- Exp. 2 (n = 64, 8 per cell): zero control, green 25% or 75% of trials. Non-depressed rated 75-75 higher (men 30.3, women 51.4) than 25-25 (20.0, 7.5); significant for women, the same direction for men (p. 458); depressed did not differ (Table 5, p. 459). "Zero control" in 75-75: 6% of non-depressed (1 of 16), 50% of depressed (p. 461).
- Typical valid reason given by participants: relative efficacy of pressing vs not pressing (p. 455).
- Replication Dev, Moore, Johnson & Garrett 2022, Collabra 8(1):38529 (preregistered; 246 + 136 people in the text, 246 + 134 in the abstract; 242 in the two zero-contingency conditions). Overestimated control under zero contingency replicated (Table 1). Higher mean at 75% than 25%, but the frequency effect was significant only for trial-level predictions, not for the end-of-task rating. The depression link did not replicate.
